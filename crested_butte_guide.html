Tonight & tomorrow

Landing at 8:35pm

You land in Gunnison after dark. The drive north to Crested Butte is about 30 miles up Highway 135, roughly 35 to 40 minutes. It is the only paved road in, well graded and easy, but it is high country at night. Watch for deer and elk on the road, keep your speed sensible, and do not count on gas or food past Gunnison once you are rolling.

The plan

Ease in, hydrate, sleep high

Tonight
Grab food in Gunnison before the drive if the flight ran long, or push straight through and land something simple in town. The Eldo on Elk Avenue keeps a late kitchen and pours the local Irwin beer. If you would rather stay in, Secret Stash pizza travels well back to the room.
Before bed
Water, not altitude heroics. You just climbed to nearly 9,000 feet. Drink more than feels necessary, go easy on the wine tonight, and expect the first night of sleep to be light. That is normal at elevation.
Tomorrow (Sat, Oct 10)
Slow morning, big coffee, short shakeout. Coffee at Camp 4 or Rumors, breakfast at McGill's, then an easy walk or gentle jog on the Woods Walk to open the legs and see the aspens. Save any real effort for day three.
First big dinner
Soupçon. Book it now. It is the town's fine dining anchor, tiny, and fills fast in fall. Aim for a night early enough in the trip to lock the reservation.
Season check: Mid October is the quiet shoulder. Peak aspen color usually runs late September into early October, so you may catch the tail of it rather than the peak, and some summer operations start winding down. The town stays open and easy, just calmer. Kebler Pass road typically closes to through traffic around mid October, so drive it early in your stay.

Altitude, honestly

Crested Butte sits near 8,900 feet and the trails climb from there. Coming from sea level, expect a lighter first sleep, a faster heart rate, and quicker dehydration. Hydrate hard, hold back on alcohol the first night or two, and do not chase efforts early. Most people feel normal by day three.

For you specifically, this is a recovery window, not a training block. Three weeks out from the Julian Alps 50K, the smart play is easy miles and letting the altitude do the intensity for you.

Driving & weather

Highway 135 is the only paved route in and it is well maintained. October can deliver the first snow of the season, so keep an eye on the forecast and check cotrip.org before mountain drives. Kebler Pass and other dirt roads can close on short notice once weather turns.

Layer for a 30 to 40 degree swing between a frosty dawn and a bluebird afternoon. Sun is intense at this elevation even when it is cold.

Section 04

Running the valley

This is a recovery-and-easy-miles trip, not a training block. You will be about three weeks past the Julian Alps 50K, and you are starting from near 9,000 feet. Let the altitude supply the stimulus and keep the effort conversational. Scenic over hard, every single day.

Signature easy

Woods Walk

Trailhead 0.2 mi up Kebler Pass Rd

The prettiest, gentlest run in town. Opens through an aspen grove to a grand vista of Peanut Lake, Gothic Mountain and the Butte. Golden in October. Perfect shakeout the morning after you land.

Easy to moderate

Lower Loop

Starts at west end of Teocalli Ave

Five miles of wide, forgiving trail that drops into public land fast and runs out toward Oh-Be-Joyful Creek. Connects to the Woods Walk if you want to link them. Aspen tunnels the whole way.

Rest-day flat

Peanut Lake Road

Northwest edge of town

Dirt road with barely any grade and huge mountain views. The one to run when you want to move without asking anything of the legs. Great with Brooke on foot too.

Altitude rules for the week: Keep the first two runs short and truly easy. Hydrate before and after every effort, add electrolytes, and expect paces to feel slower at this elevation, which is exactly right. If you feel a headache or poor sleep the first nights, that is the altitude, so hold the intensity down until it clears.

Section 06

The drive: Kebler Pass

Your one essential outing. Kebler Pass runs 30 miles of dirt west from Crested Butte through the Gunnison National Forest, home to one of the largest aspen groves on earth, over 45,000 trees on a single root system. In October it turns solid gold.

Do it early in the trip

A golden half-day loop

Timing
Go in the first days of your stay. The gravel road typically closes to through traffic around mid October once weather turns, and you land Oct 9. Aim for a clear, dry morning.
The drive
Roughly 2 hours one way at 30 mph on gravel. Grades are gentle, but it is a dirt mountain road. Any rental handles it in dry conditions. Turn back if it is snowing or muddy.
Run it
Park at a pullout near Lost Lake or Horse Ranch Park and run an easy out-and-back through the aspens. Keep it short and conversational given the elevation. This is the scenic long-run substitute: golden tunnels, no traffic, all effort optional.
Back in town
Reward with lunch on Elk Avenue. Teocalli Tamale or a Secret Stash slice, then coffee at Camp 4.
Luxe side option: Brooke's Four Seasons booking (Oct 13 to 17) is not in Crested Butte, there is no Four Seasons here. The nearest is Four Seasons Vail, about a 3.5 to 4 hour drive north and east. If the plan is a couple of nights of full luxury there, treat Vail as a separate luxe leg rather than a day trip, and keep Crested Butte the mountain-town base.
