The First Weekend

You arrive Thursday October 29 into a city that is about to host a Formula 1 Grand Prix and Day of the Dead in the same 72 hours. This is either the best weekend of the whole year of travel or a logistical disaster, and the difference is entirely down to what you book in advance.

Motorsport

F1 Mexico City Grand Prix · Oct 30 to Nov 1

Round 20 of the championship, at the Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez in Iztacalco, with the race on Sunday November 1. The stadium section through the old baseball diamond is the loudest corner in Formula 1 and the atmosphere is regularly voted the best on the calendar. Grandstand tickets sell out months ahead but resale is reliable.

Take the Metro to Ciudad Deportiva. Driving to that circuit on race day is a two hour mistake.
The Reason People Come

Día de Muertos · Oct 31 to Nov 2

Three separate processions run in the week: the Alebrijes parade in mid October, the Mega Procesion de Catrinas in late October, and the grand Desfile de Dia de Muertos itself, which runs from the Puerta de los Leones in Chapultepec down Paseo de la Reforma to the Zocalo. Reported 2026 dates differ between November 1 and November 2, so confirm on the city government site once it publishes.

The Zocalo ofrenda is enormous and free. Go at night.
The Real One

Mixquic, the night of November 1

The parade is a modern invention created after a James Bond film. The genuine tradition is in San Andres Mixquic in the far southeast of the city, where the whole town spends the night of November 1 in the churchyard with candles, marigolds and their families. It is quiet, overwhelming and the most moving thing you will see in Mexico. Hire a driver, go late, stay late.

Ninety minutes each way. Worth every minute.

How to sequence it

Thu 29
Land and do nothing. You are at 2,240 metres. Eat lightly, drink water, walk Condesa, sleep early. Altitude arrives on day two, not day one.
Fri 30
F1 practice if you have tickets, otherwise the Anthropology Museum in the morning and Xochimilco altars in the afternoon.
Sat 31
Qualifying, then the Catrinas and street celebrations across Roma and Centro at night. The city dresses up and the streets fill.
Sun 1
Race day, then Mixquic overnight if you have the legs for both. If not, pick one and do it properly.
Mon 2
Recover. Coyoacan cemetery in the afternoon, an early dinner, nothing ambitious.
Nov 3 to 10
The real stay. The city empties out, restaurants become bookable, and this becomes one of the best cities in the world to just live in for a week.

Travel Basics

The altitude is the thing nobody warns you about properly, and it will affect every run you do for the first week.

Currency
Peso $
1 USD buys about 17.0 MXN as of late August 2026. Written with a dollar sign, which catches everyone out. Cards widely accepted, cash for markets and taxis.
Elevation
2,240 m
7,350 feet. Expect around 12 percent off your threshold pace for the first ten days, and worse sleep for the first three nights.
Weather
23 °C day
Dry season starts in November. Cool mornings near 10 °C, warm dry afternoons, strong sun. Almost no rain after early November.
Getting around
Uber and Didi
Both excellent, cheap and safe. Uber is the default. Never hail a street taxi. Metro is fine in daylight but avoid it with luggage.
Air quality
Check IMECA
November is usually good but inversions happen. Check the index before a hard session and move it indoors on bad days.
Water
Bottled only
Do not drink the tap water and do not use it to brush teeth. Every apartment has a garrafon delivery.

Chilango vocabulary

Chilango a Mexico City native ¡Qué padre! how cool Ahorita in a moment, meaning any time ¿Mande? sorry, what? Chido cool, good Neta really, truly Provecho said to anyone eating Antojito street snack Garnacha fried street food La cuenta the bill Ofrenda the Day of the Dead altar Cempasúchil the marigold

Safety and practicalities

  • Roma Norte, Condesa, Polanco, Juarez and Coyoacan are safe and walkable day and night. Stay in that footprint and you will have no problems.
  • Use Uber rather than street taxis, always. This is the single biggest safety lever in the city.
  • Phone snatching happens on busy streets and on the Metro. Keep it away when walking.
  • Do not carry more cash than you need and use ATMs inside banks or malls.
  • Earthquake drills sound a citywide alarm and are common. Learn where the safe zone is in your building on day one.
  • Tipping is 10 to 15 percent in restaurants, and card machines will ask you at the table.

Section 4

Running

Twelve days at 7,350 feet is a genuine training stimulus if you handle it right, and a hole you fall into if you do not.

The altitude rules

Days one to three, easy only, no exceptions, and expect the effort to feel wrong. Days four to seven, add strides and short hills. From day eight you can run a real session. Hydrate far beyond normal because the air is dry as well as thin, and add electrolytes to everything. If sleep is broken for the first three nights, that is the altitude and it passes.

The daily loops

Parque Mexico and Parque Espana in Condesa give you a soft one and a half kilometre circuit with a running track surface, and it is where the whole neighbourhood runs at 7am. Bosque de Chapultepec is the big one, over 600 hectares with a dedicated running circuit around the first section, the lake and the Anthropology Museum. Twelve kilometres inside Chapultepec without repeating yourself is easy. On Sundays, Paseo de la Reforma closes to traffic for the Muevete en Bici ciclovia from 8am to 2pm, giving you kilometres of empty boulevard.

The long run: Desierto de los Leones

A pine forest national park in the mountains southwest of the city, starting around 2,800 metres and climbing. Trails, cool air, and total silence forty minutes from Condesa by Uber. The loop from the old Carmelite monastery up towards Cruz Blanca and back gives you twenty to twenty five kilometres on soft forest singletrack with serious vertical. Go early, go with company, and take everything you need because there is nothing up there.

Afternoons

One good thing a day, building from light to memorable, and skewed towards what only exists here.

Light

Museo Nacional de Antropología

Chapultepec

One of the greatest museums on earth and the single best three hours in Mexico City. The Aztec and Maya halls alone justify the trip. Go Tuesday morning.

Light

Casa Luis Barragán

Tacubaya

The house of Mexico's greatest architect, a UNESCO site, and a lesson in colour and light. Tiny group tours, book weeks ahead online.

Doctores

Friday nights. Masks, beer, families, absolute chaos. Buy ringside, take nothing valuable, and stay for the main event. Nothing else in the world is like it.

Section 6

Weekend Side Trip: Valle de Bravo

Two and a half hours west, 1,800 metres, a lake ringed by pine forest, and the best long run terrain within reach of the capital.

Getting there

Two and a half hours by car on the Toluca highway, and a hired driver for the weekend is straightforward and not expensive. Do not drive yourself back on a Sunday evening, the return traffic into the city is genuinely brutal. The alternative is Tepoztlan, ninety minutes south, smaller and with a much harder climb up the Tepozteco.

The long run: the Valle ridge trails

Valle de Bravo sits at about 1,830 metres, four hundred metres lower than the city, which means you will feel fast. The trail network above the town towards La Peña and the monarch butterfly reserve country gives you long soft climbs on forest singletrack. A twenty five kilometre loop out along the lake road, up through the pines and back down into town is straightforward to build, with two thousand feet of climbing and pine needle underfoot the whole way.

The rest of the weekend

Stay at Rodavento or El Santuario on the lake, both with proper spas. Paraglide off Monte Alto, which is one of the best flying sites in the world and takes twenty minutes to arrange. Eat at Casa Rodavento or La Michoacana in town. And if the timing works in early November, the monarch butterfly reserves at El Rosario and Sierra Chincua begin opening for the season, an hour and a half further north.

Luxury Shopping

Polanco is the luxury spine, and Roma is where you find things you genuinely cannot buy anywhere else.

The Golden Mile

Avenida Presidente Masaryk

Polanco

Mexico's most expensive street. Hermès, Louis Vuitton, Cartier, Tiffany, Gucci, Bulgari, all in three walkable blocks with good restaurants between them.

Mall

Antara and Palacio de Hierro Polanco

Polanco

Antara is the open air luxury mall. Palacio de Hierro is the great Mexican department store and its Polanco flagship is the best single building for shopping in the country.

Concept

Common People

Polanco

A concept store in an old mansion, with international designers, Mexican labels, books and a cafe. The best curated retail in the city.

Mexican Design

Carla Fernández and Lago

Roma Norte

Carla Fernandez works directly with indigenous artisan communities and the results are extraordinary. Lago DF does modern Mexican womenswear. Both are pieces you cannot get at home.

Mexican Design

Onora and Casa Caballeria

Polanco

Onora sells contemporary homeware made by Mexican craftspeople. Caballeria does leather and menswear with real quality.

Craft

Ciudadela Mercado de Artesanías

Centro

Every Mexican craft tradition under one roof. Oaxacan textiles, Talavera, alebrijes, silver from Taxco. Bargain politely and buy from the stalls that name their village.

Vintage

Bazar de Oro and Roma vintage

Roma Norte

Colima and Cordoba streets have a dozen small vintage and design shops. Best hunting in the city for mid century Mexican furniture and objects.

Sunday

Bazar Sábado, San Ángel

San Ángel

Saturdays only, in a colonial house in the prettiest neighbourhood in the city. The highest quality craft market in Mexico. Go early and stay for lunch.
